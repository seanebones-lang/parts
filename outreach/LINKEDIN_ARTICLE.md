## Body (copy-paste ready)

Every fixed-ops leader I talk to has the same ghost in the building.

A customer—or an advisor—asks a simple question:

**“Do you have brake pads for a 2019 Honda Civic?”**

What should be a ten-second answer turns into a scavenger hunt: one screen in the DMS, a ping to another rooftop, a callback, maybe a hopeful “we should have those.” Sometimes you’re right. Sometimes you’re not—and the comeback costs more than the pad set.

### The wrong kind of AI

The market is full of chat windows that write confident paragraphs.

Confidence is not inventory.

If a model invents stock, it isn’t “innovative.” It’s a liability with a friendly tone of voice. Dealers don’t need more theater. They need **answers they can defend at the parts counter.**

### What we built instead

At **NextEleven**, we built **Parts**: multi-location dealership parts intelligence.

You type the question the way a human asks it. The system:

1. **Retrieves** candidates with hybrid search (semantic + keyword-style ranking)  
2. **Scores** them across locations  
3. Returns a **green / yellow / red** traffic-light so your team knows when AI is enough—and when a human must take the wheel  

Optional language models can help **phrase** an answer. They are not the source of truth for whether the box is on the shelf.

### Why multi-location is the point

Single-store search is table stakes.

Groups win or lose on **rooftop-aware** availability: the pad set at Wrigley vs North vs the store across town. Parts is built around that reality—same catalog model, location-aware ranking, and the ability to expand a hit across sibling locations for the same base SKU.

### Trust is a product feature

We obsess over a boring word: **policy**.

- Strong match + healthy stock → **green**  
- Ambiguous match or thin stock → **yellow**  
- Weak match or zero stock → **red**  

That isn’t a gimmick for a slide deck. It’s how you introduce AI into a culture that has every reason to be skeptical.

### What a serious pilot looks like

We don’t start with “replace your DMS.”

We start with **your top twenty questions**, a measured hit-rate, latency you can feel at the counter, and an override rate you can live with. Thirty days. Clear exit criteria. Proprietary software—no open-source surprise, no training free-for-all on your catalog without an agreement.

### The punchline

AI in fixed ops shouldn’t feel like magic.

It should feel like the best parts person on your team—fast across every store, honest when they’re unsure, and never bluffing a customer to look smart.

If you run multi-rooftop parts and you’re tired of phone trees disguised as process, let’s talk about a measured pilot.
